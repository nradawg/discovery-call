**Use this when** you have a call booked and you need to find out whether a
real deal exists here, before you spend a month pitching one that does not.

## What I do

I write down the answer that would make you walk away. Then I write the
questions that could actually produce it, and I delete every question that
cannot. A question with no disqualifying answer is a question you are asking to
feel good.

This is not a pitch plan. The job of this call is to earn the right to spend
time, or to get the hour back.

## The test every question has to pass

Draft the call twice, with opposite answers to the question. If both drafts end
with you sending a proposal, the question is decoration. Cut it.

| cut | keep |
|---|---|
| What are your goals this year | What happens if you do nothing until January |
| Who is involved in decisions | Who signed the last thing like this |
| What is your budget | What is the money currently being spent on instead |
| Are you looking to improve X | What did you try before me, and why did it stop |

The keep column can all come back with an answer that ends the deal. That is
the only property that matters.

## The four that disqualify

Most non-deals die on one of these, and all four are askable in a first call:

1. **Pain with a cost.** Not "that is annoying." A number, an hour count, a
   person's time, a lost customer. If they cannot name what it costs, nothing
   is broken enough to buy.
2. **Doing nothing.** What happens in six months if they never talk to you
   again. If the answer is "about the same," there is no deal, there is an
   interest.
3. **The signature.** Who signed the last purchase of roughly this size. Past
   tense, because the present tense gets you an org chart instead of a name.
4. **The date, and what drives it.** A date with no driver behind it is a wish.
   Ask what happens on that date.

Known frameworks cover similar ground under their own acronyms. The acronym is
not the point, the disqualifying answer is, and a framework filled in
completely can still describe a deal that does not exist.

## What you see

```
CALL: Dana Whitaker, Ops Manager, Harbor Line Freight, 30 minutes

WALK AWAY IF
  Dispatch is annoying but costs nothing countable, and no date exists.
  Then this is a referral source, not a deal, and you say so on the call.

WHAT I ALREADY THINK I KNOW
  Hiring   Two dispatcher roles open, 9 and 22 days old.
           CHECKED, their careers page today.
  Pain     Dispatcher rerouting by phone.  GUESSED from those two roles.
  Cost     Unknown. Nothing in hand. This is question one.
  Signer   GUESSED. Her title suggests it, nothing confirms it.
  Date     Unknown. Possibly tied to filling the open seats.  GUESSED.
  Booked   She agreed to 30 minutes on the phone herself.
           FROM YOU, you said "she picked the time."
  Tried    Nothing known.  Open question.

ASK, IN THIS ORDER
  1  How many times a day does a driver call in for a new address?
  2  What does that cost you, in hours or in a late delivery?
  3  If nothing changes by spring, what breaks first?
  4  Last time you bought software for the floor, who signed it?
  5  What did you try before, and why did you stop?
  6  Is there a date anything has to be live by, and what is on that date?

DO NOT ASK
  What are your goals for the year        no disqualifying answer
  What is your budget for this            you get a made up range
  Would better scheduling help            yes, always, useless

KILL SIGNALS, SAY IT OUT LOUD IF YOU HEAR THEM
  "We would just need to find time to look at it"       no driver
  "I would bring it to the owner"                       no signature
  "It is more of a nice to have"                        no cost

5 unknowns going in. 4 of them are the ones that decide this.
```

## Limits

I cannot hear the answers, and the real signal on this call is tone, not
content. People also describe their own authority generously, so a confident
"I decide this" is data, not proof. And a call can pass all four and still die
for a reason nobody mentioned.

The trap: happy ears, and the naive version builds them in. Ask only questions
with pleasant answers and every call sounds like a deal, so the pipeline fills
with warm nothing and you find out in month three. If this call has no
disqualifying question in it, you did not run discovery, you ran a demo with
extra steps.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
